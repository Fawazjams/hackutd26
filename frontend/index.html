        The market

        What downtime costs

        Each hour a GPU is down costs its share of the H100 market price that day, scaled by how busy the hall is at that hour.

        Real weekly prices through Nov 2, daily forecast is our model. Data: GetDeploying, CC BY 4.0, forecast added.
        Method

        How it’s priced

        Dj(s) = s + Lj − 1Σh = s Nj · u(h) · p(⌊h/24⌋)
        (10)
        Downtime cost of job j started at hour s: N GPUs offline for L hours, each busy hour worth that day’s H100 price.
        Sj = κ · σ · Dj
        (11)
        κ = 0.70 contracted share, σ = 0.10 SLA credit, so S = 7% of D
        Credits owed on contracted hours missed. Both values assumed.
        πj(s) = min( 1, rj · s24 )
        (12)
        Failure chance grows by r (0.5% to 2% per day, by job, assumed) for every day the job waits.
        Ej = (1 − πj) Pj + πj Uj = Pj + Rj
        (13)
        P = D + S planned, U = 3 × 1.2 × P unplanned, R = π (U − P)
        An unplanned failure costs 3× the planned outage plus a 20% emergency premium (assumed), priced at the week’s average rate P.
        Tz(h+1) = Tz(h) + 0.6 · ( Tz*(h) − Tz(h) )
        (14)
        Overheat when Tz > 32 °C (ASHRAE A1 allowable inlet limit)
        Each cooling zone moves 60% of the way to its target temperature every hour. The target rises when the zone’s own cooling is down, more if a neighbour’s is down too.
        O = ΣTz(h) > 32 Nzon(h) · u(h) · p(d) · 3.6 · (1 + κσ)
        (15)
        Every overheated zone-hour is priced as an unplanned outage of the GPUs still running in that zone. Only ASAP has any.

        Downtime is lost GPU-cloud revenue, and the SLA term adds credits owed on contracted hours.

        The planner tries every allowed start for every task, never overlaps two jobs in one cooling zone, and keeps the cheapest schedule. The baseline starts each job at the first business-hours slot.
